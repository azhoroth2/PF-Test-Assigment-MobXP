⚠️ 8 blockers identified across Foreman + HR Manager flows

Blocker 1 · Worker
Missed clock-out propagates silently
👷 Worker → 🔧 Foreman → 📋 HR → 💰 Payroll
No record is created. Error travels downstream invisibly until it surfaces pre-payroll — when fixing it costs the most time.
System timer detects missed clock-out after shift hours exceeded. Batches into Foreman summary. Phase 2: geofence exit notification.
Blocker 2 · Foreman
Notification at worst possible moment
🔧 Foreman — end of shift
Traditional: alert fires at shift end — during handover, safety checks, equipment shutdown. Notification ignored in factory floor noise.
Delay notification 30 min post-shift. Batch ALL anomalies into one shift summary. Foreman acts post-chaos, not mid-chaos.
Blocker 3 · Foreman
Factory floor connectivity + app friction
🔧 Foreman — mobile on factory floor
WiFi dead zones. App on personal phone. Complex UI = abandoned process. One thumb free at best.
Offline-capable mobile UI. Critical path = one notification → one list → single-tap action. No multi-step navigation.
Blocker 4 · Foreman
Knowledge gap — Foreman approves blind
🔧 Foreman — manual correction review
Managed 20 workers, not one. Doesn't know exact departure times. May rubber-stamp AI suggestions or skip entirely. 5+ anomalies = abandoned.
AI confidence score signals when auto-approve is safe vs. when real review needed. Pre-defined tap reasons — zero typing on floor.
Blocker 5 · HR
HR approves corrections blind — legal risk
📋 HR Manager — corrections dashboard
Foreman skipped notes. HR sees AI suggestion with no context. Approving = signing off legally. Wrong correction → PIP audit liability.
AI reasoning trail: "Suggested 16:00 — matches shift schedule, 94% historical match." HR acknowledges reasoning explicitly, not just clicks approve.
Blocker 6 · HR
AI numbers can't be defended at PIP audit
📋 HR Manager — pre-payroll AI summary
"The system calculated it" is not a legal defense. HR is personally liable for compliance accuracy under Polish labor law.
Every flag expandable to source data + calculation. Compliance-ready audit log auto-generated. HR must acknowledge each flag before export unlocks.
Blocker 7 · HR
Return-to-Foreman creates unresolved loop
📋 HR → 🔧 Foreman — clarification loop
Foreman is running next shift. Doesn't respond quickly. Correction sits unresolved. Payroll deadline approaching. HR stuck: approve blind or hold payroll.
24h SLA on corrections. After deadline: auto-escalation with AI recommendation. HR approves with documented note. No manual chase.
Blocker 8 · HR + Payroll
Export errors surface at worst moment
📋 HR → 💰 Payroll Admin — export
Payroll Admin finds format issues or missing data after receiving the export. Payroll run is imminent. Correction loop back to HR under maximum pressure.
Pre-built Optima/enova365 export format (PF structural moat). Pre-export validation: all flags acknowledged before export button unlocks.

🤖 AI Solution Canvas — 3 inferential features (rules elsewhere)

Feature 1 · Confidence Score
When to bulk-approve vs. inspect
🤖 Foreman correction · Screen 2
Fixed-site suggested time is a deterministic RULE. The inference is the score — over 4 signals: worker history, that-day clock-in, pre-approved OT flag, team pattern. Seeded by historical import at onboarding (no cold start).
Conflicting signals → lower score → routed to human. Threshold customer-tunable but every change logged (who/when) for audit defensibility. False high-confidence is the expensive failure — model biases conservative.
Feature 2 · Anomaly Detection
Who reaches the flagged list
🤖 +30 min post-shift · Screen 1
Flags 4 types: missing clock-out, missing clock-in, implausible duration, unapproved OT. Over-flag kills adoption; under-flag misses real gaps.
Two-speed by confidence: high → Foreman now; low → held, rolled into HR pre-payroll summary as 'review if time allows'. Nothing dropped; each anomaly lands at the right desk. Foreman tier learns within 24h, HR tier at period-end.
Feature 3 · Pre-payroll Summary · HERO
Synthesis & audit-defensibility
🤖 HR pre-payroll · Screen 4
Hundreds of events → a handful of acknowledged, audit-ready flags that unlock export. Ranked job: defensibility is the floor, speed optimized on top — never at its expense.
Every flag + money claim (e.g. night premium PLN 847) expandable to workers · hours · rate · legal rule. Acknowledgement requires the calculation was shown. Export locked until done. Danger isn't a wrong number — it's unexamined sign-off.

Design spine: every AI feature is calibrated to make false high-confidence the most expensive failure — then engineered to avoid it. F1 routes ambiguity to a human, F2 routes uncertain anomalies to the right desk, F3 gates acknowledgement on shown calculation. The system never optimizes for AI being right most often — it optimizes for AI being never confidently wrong in a way a human rubber-stamps. Final approval is never AI. Permit / right-to-work validation moved to the compliance layer, out of this flow.

🔮 8 validation points — unvalidated assumptions + hypotheses to test

Each ‘V’ badge marks a place where the design rests on an assumption we have not yet validated. Ordered by de-risk priority — V1 and V4 are the riskiest (they can invalidate the whole problem or the AI).

V1 · MissedCO · riskiest
Is missed clock-out the core problem?
Problem-level assumption
We assume missed clock-outs are frequent enough to justify the whole correction layer — not a rare edge case dwarfed by leave/contract disputes.
H: missed clock-outs ≥40% of corrections. Data audit + Foreman diary (3 sites, 2 wks). Disconfirm → problem selection wrong.
V4 · AISuggest · riskiest
Is the confidence score trustworthy?
AI calibration assumption
We assume schedule + 4 signals produce a calibrated, defensible score. Calibration is unproven — and a confidently-wrong score is the expensive failure.
H: ≥90%-confidence corrections right ≥95%. Backtest on import data + worker confirmation. Disconfirm → raise threshold / change signals.
V3 · D_Tier
Will Foremen trust the two-speed split?
Adoption assumption
We assume Foremen bulk-approve high-confidence flags without inspecting each — the mechanism the adoption case rests on.
H: bulk-approve in ≤2 min, no later distrust. Moderated task + trust interview. Disconfirm → time-saving collapses.
V7 · HRSumm
Does the audit trail create real confidence?
Trust assumption
We assume expandable calculation makes HR feel confident to sign off — vs. still exposed, or just rubber-stamping.
H: higher self-reported confidence + genuine expand-before-ack. Comparative test + click-depth logging. Disconfirm → trust is fake.
V6 · D_SLA
Is a 24h SLA realistic?
Process assumption
We assume a Foreman running the next shift resolves corrections within 24h without constant escalation.
H: ≥70% resolved in 24h without escalation. Pilot SLA tracking. Disconfirm → SLA is theater, redesign handoff.
V2 · SysDetect
Is +30 min the right delay?
Timing assumption
We assume 30 min post-shift lets the Foreman act post-chaos. Could be too soon (still busy) or too late (gone home).
H: ≥3 of 5 act on a 30-min batch same evening. Timing A/B (0/30/60). Disconfirm → format is the lever, not timing.
V5 · ManualRev
Do tap reasons cover reality?
Taxonomy assumption
We assume the fixed reason set covers real causes — so no typing is ever needed on the floor.
H: fixed reasons cover ≥85%, <15% need free-text. Pilot reason-frequency log. Disconfirm → taxonomy wrong, HR loses context.
V8 · ExportRep
Does pre-built format kill export errors?
Integration assumption
We assume the pre-built Optima/enova365 format + validation means zero payroll-admin errors. Real-world edge cases untested.
H: ≥95% of exports pass first time. Pilot round-trip count (3 sites). Disconfirm → validation rules incomplete.
